Premise, setting & tone

§1

In the prompt

  • Charter opens on Knox County, Kentucky, July 1993, the first weeks of the Knox Event.
  • The quarantine in full: third day of the exclusion, telephones dead a week, General John McGrew's noon statement on 9 July, the official "non-lethal" line nobody inside believes.
  • The 1993 texture — no mobiles, no internet, a television is furniture.
  • A strong instruction that the gap between the broadcast and the street is the tone.

Not in the prompt

  • The thesis. "This is how you died" — the game's own one-line premise — appears nowhere.
  • A start date that can move. The calendar is asserted as fact and is actually a player setting.
  • Any reconciliation between the fixed 9 July timeline and how long the survivor has actually been alive.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1ToneSource read

The narrator does not know the story has one ending

This is the largest omission in the audit and the cheapest to fix. Project Zomboid's entire authorial stance is one sentence, and the charter never says it. The narrator has been told it is writing a survival story; it has not been told it is writing a tragedy — a book whose last chapter is already written and whose only open question is how long the middle runs.

That single fact changes what a page is for. It is why a found tin of beans is poignant rather than a win, why a fixed door is worth a paragraph, why the last beat should be a want the reader suspects will not be granted. Without it the narrator defaults to the neutral register of adventure fiction, which is exactly the flatness that has been showing up in the pages.

Cost: one paragraph in Prompt.CHARTER. Care needed: it must forbid foreshadowing this particular death and forbid promising rescue — the doom is a colour, not a plot. And because you have ruled out hardcoded cardinal rules, this belongs behind a Settings dropdown alongside the spoiler dial: Inevitable / Ambiguous / Hopeful, defaulting to Inevitable.
Done: Prompt.tone() now ships a WHAT KIND OF BOOK THIS IS block in the cached system prefix, behind a new SETUP dial — might be won / nobody knows / this is how you died, defaulting to the last. All three variants forbid foreshadowing the actual death.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 2CorrectnessApocalypse.luajavap

The prompt asserts a calendar the player can change

World.KNOX states flatly that "the save's first day is 9 July 1993" and builds the whole Knox Knews timeline on it. That is the Apocalypse default — I confirmed it in media/lua/shared/Sandbox/Apocalypse.lua: StartYear = 1, StartMonth = 7, StartDay = 9. But it is a setting, not a law. SandboxOptions exposes startYear, startMonth, startDay and startTime, and a player who starts in October gets a system block asserting July and a state block reporting October.

That is a Rule 1 violation we manufactured ourselves — the one thing the charter says breaks the world irreparably, introduced by our own prompt rather than by the model.

Fix: read the four start options in StateReader.sandbox(), and rewrite the KNOX timeline to be relative — "day N since the exclusion began" — computed from GameTime.getWorldAgeDaysSinceBegin(). This has a second payoff: the timeline currently goes stale after week one, because everything in it is dated absolutely and nothing tells the narrator how far past those dates the survivor now is.
Done: Solved by removing the assertion rather than reading the option. World.KNOX is now dated from “the first morning”, the snapshot carries time.daysSinceItBegan, and the Knox Knews dates are explicitly flagged as printed on the papers and therefore fixed.

Map geography & regional hubs

§2

In the prompt

  • Seven places named: Muldraugh, West Point, Rosewood, Riverside, March Ridge, Ekron, Louisville.
  • The road scenario uses the same list as a route, with Louisville held back as the far end.
  • A firm NAMES rule: no town may be named unless the state supplies it.

Not in the prompt

  • Brandenburg, Echo Creek, Fallas Lake, Irvington, Valley Station — five real B42 settlements.
  • Any sense of direction, distance, or how the towns sit relative to each other.
  • In practice, any working place name at all — see below.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 2Coveragemedia/maps survey

Five settlements exist that the narrator has never heard of

Your install's media/maps/ holds eleven map packs: Brandenburg, Echo Creek, Ekron, Fallas Lake, Irvington, March Ridge, Muldraugh, Riverside, Rosewood, Valley Station and West Point. The prompt names six of them. Irvington is a nice illustration of the cost — it appears in our Knox Knews entry for 2 July as the town where a woman survived a crash, so the narrator knows something happened there but does not know it is a place on the map the survivor could walk into.

Also checked, and it holds: I went looking for Louisville because the road scenario stakes its ending on it and B42 was widely reported to have cut it. It is still there — media/maps/Muldraugh, KY/regions.lua carries live Louisville region rows (several others commented out). The spine is safe.
Done: Brandenburg, Irvington, Echo Creek, Fallas Lake and Valley Station added to THE COUNTY.
Still openPriority 2Dead featurejavap

placeName is a rule with almost nothing behind it

The charter tells the narrator it may name a town only when position.placeName supplies one. That reads as a safe constraint. In practice it is close to a permanent gag, because IsoMetaGrid.getZoneAt() returns zones — Nav, Forest, TownZone, parking — whose getName() is usually empty or a bare type string. It almost never returns "Muldraugh".

The map's real settlement naming lives in regions.lua as named coordinate rectangles, and I checked for an engine accessor to reach it: IsoMetaGrid, IsoMetaCell and IsoWorld have none. IsoWorld.getSpawnRegion() is the only region-shaped call and it returns where the character spawned, not where they are standing.

Fix: parse regions.lua into a static coordinate table at build time — roughly forty lines — and emit position.region beside the existing fields. Until then every page set in Muldraugh has to call it "the town", which is why the prose has felt placeless.

Physiological simulation & trauma

§3

This is the strongest area in the audit, and the World.RULES block shipped in 1.14.0 closed most of what was open. Per-part reporting is genuinely good: only non-pristine parts are emitted, pain and stiffness are carried separately from health so a strain is visible, and bandage type and splints come through.

In the prompt

  • All 24 CharacterStat values — hunger, thirst, fatigue, endurance, panic, stress, boredom and the rest.
  • Per-part: health, pain, stiffness, bitten, scratched, bleeding, stemmed, deep wound, burn, bandage type, splint.
  • infected vs fakeInfected, with the excellent rule that the survivor cannot tell the two apart even though the state can.
  • Nutrition split from hunger, with an explicit warning not to dramatise calories.
  • Bite-vs-scratch, bandage hygiene, stitches, splints, pain outlasting health, sleep debt, food poisoning.

Not in the prompt

  • Every moodle. All 26 types, entirely unread.
  • Glass lodged in a wound, and whether a wound has been stitched.
  • Whether a scratch came from a window or a zombie — which is the whole question.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1Highest value / lowest costjavap

The game already decides what is worth noticing, and we throw it away

A moodle is the game's own judgement that a raw number has crossed into being worth a person's attention — expressed in tiers, in the player's own vocabulary. We send the narrator stress: 0.41 and make it invent a threshold. The game will simply tell us "Stressed", level 2.

Confirmed reachable: IsoGameCharacter.getMoodles() returns a Moodles with getMoodleLevel(MoodleType), getMoodleDisplayString(MoodleType) and getMoodleDescriptionString(MoodleType). MoodleType registers 26 constants:

ENDURANCE TIRED HUNGRY PANIC SICK BORED UNHAPPY BLEEDING WET HAS_A_COLD ANGRY STRESS THIRST INJURED PAIN HEAVY_LOAD DRUNK DEAD ZOMBIE HYPERTHERMIA HYPOTHERMIA WINDCHILL CANT_SPRINT UNCOMFORTABLE NOXIOUS_SMELL FOOD_EATEN

The nine picked out above are invisible to us today — no stat we read exposes them. Three of those nine are the exact conditions World.RULES instructs the narrator to dramatise: it says "being soaked and cold is genuinely dangerous in the wrong season" while giving the narrator no way to know the survivor is wet, chilled, or standing in wind.

Fix: one loop over MoodleType, emitting name and level for anything above zero. Perhaps thirty lines. It also does something the charter has been fighting for since the first build — "numbers are yours to feel, not to quote" is currently enforced against a state block that contains nothing but numbers. Moodles hand the narrator the feeling directly.
Done: StateReader.feeling() reflects over the MoodleType registry and emits every active moodle with its display label and level, under a note telling the narrator these outrank the raw stats. Mod-registered moodles come through for free.
Still openPriority 3Detailjavap

Three wound details worth one line each

BodyPart exposes haveGlass(), stitched() and getStitchTime() — glass still in the wound and a wound that has been sewn are both concrete, and both are the kind of thing a page notices. More interesting: SetScratchedWindow() and SetScratchedWeapon() mean the engine distinguishes a scratch taken climbing through glass from one taken off a zombie. That distinction is the entire content of the survivor's dread, and we do not read it.

Checked and absent: no per-part bullet-lodged accessor exists in 42.20.3 — I looked. Lodged bullets are not a thing the state can report, so the checklist item is answered "not available" rather than "not done".

Character build architecture

§4
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1Prose qualityjavap

The narrator is handed a list of trait names and asked to guess

The charter's WHO THEY ARE block is one of the best things in the prompt — biography not statistics, never name a trait, a tendency rather than a compulsion. It is asked to do that work on a bare array of strings.

For "Claustrophobic" that is fine. For Thin-skinned, Conspicuous, Desensitized, Fast Learner, Adrenaline Junkie, Crafty, Artisan the narrator has to infer both what the trait does and — crucially — whether it is a virtue or a flaw. It will get some of them wrong, and a page that treats a burden as a gift misreads the character at the root.

All of it is one static call away. CharacterTraitDefinition.getCharacterTraitDefinition(trait) yields getUIName(), getDescription(), getXpBoosts() and getCost() — and cost is the polarity: positive means the player spent points on it, negative means they took it to buy something else.

Deliberately not recommended: the numeric point budget itself. It is character-creation UI, not a fact about the world, and putting arithmetic in the prompt invites exactly the statistic-quoting the charter bans. Ship polarity and description; leave the ledger out. Occupation deserves the same treatment — right now it is a bare name, and getPerkBoost() would say what the trade actually made them good at, which is the concrete grounding under "an electrician counts outlets".
Done: Traits are now objects, not strings: name, UI name, description, and a polarity read off getCost() — “a strength, chosen and paid for” against “a weakness, taken on purpose”. The point budget was deliberately left out.

Skill progression & knowledge acquisition

§5

The weakest subject against the checklist, and the one where your own earlier instruction — "watching TV was important, but only to level 3" — is still not actually encoded anywhere.

Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1Verified scheduleRadioData.xml

Life and Living stops teaching on day 8, and nothing in the prompt knows it

I pulled channel 203 out of media/radio/RadioData.xml on your machine and counted every XP code in it. This is not recalled from a wiki; it is the file the game reads.

Life and Living TV · Television · freq 203 COO+1 61 lines Cooking CRP+1 53 lines Carpentry FIS+1 17 lines Fishing FOR+1 12 lines Foraging TRA+1 8 lines Trapping FRM+1 6 lines Farming BOR-1 267 lines boredom relief STS-1 5 lines stress relief RCP= Make Cake Batter · Make Fishing Rod Make Pie Dough · Make Crate Trap Daily shape morning cooking · midday carpentry evening rotates through the rest XP grants end after day 8. So there is a hard eight-day window, it is front-loaded, and it closes. That is real narrative pressure of exactly the kind the story engine exists to dramatise, and the narrator currently has no idea the television is anything but furniture.

The charter half-gestures at this already — "mains power and broadcast television are temporary and are worth noticing while they last" — but frames it purely as atmosphere. It never connects the set to learning, so the narrator notices a working television as a small miracle and not as a resource with a clock on it.

Fix: a short block in World.KNOX carrying the schedule above, plus a line in World.RULES that reading and watching are how skill is actually acquired. Add the outbreak-day count from §1 and the narrator can feel the window closing without ever being allowed to say so.
Done: The schedule is in World.KNOX as its own block, framed as what a working set is worth in the first week rather than as a thing to go and do. World.RULES gained a LEARNING paragraph covering the five graded volumes and the television together.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 2Missing signaljavap

Skill books are inventory, not progression

Volume 1–5 books are the whole mid-game learning loop, and the snapshot reports only a title and a [read] / [unread] flag. Whether the volume matches the current level, and whether a multiplier is live right now, are both readable: IsoGameCharacter$XP exposes getMultiplier(Perk) and getPerkBoost(Perk).

Delta.skills() already reports level-ups well, which makes the gap sharper — the narrator is told a skill went up and given nothing about why. A carpentry level that arrived off a book reads differently from one that arrived off a hammer, and today they are indistinguishable.

Done: The skills block now carries fromTheirTrade from getPerkBoost() and a learningFaster note from getMultiplier(), so a level that arrived off a book reads differently from one earned with a hammer.

World degradation & infrastructure collapse

§6

In the prompt

  • The ATTRITION paragraph: batteries die, petrol goes stale, tools break, every shop is emptier than the last.
  • Weather in full — temperature, light, rain, snow, fog, wind.
  • The One Year spine leans on stores, seasons and the leaking roof.

Not in the prompt

  • Whether the power is on right now. One method call.
  • Whether the water is on right now. Also one method call.
  • The shutoff days, which the sandbox knows.
  • Erosion — for a scenario whose arc is time.
  • Generators, rain collectors, fuel, plumbing. The narrator does not know generators exist.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1CorrectnessjavapApocalypse.lua

"Nobody knows the day" is not true — the save does

World.RULES closes with "the mains power and water WILL fail; nobody knows the day." The second half is wrong, and it is wrong in a way that costs us the best slow-dread device in the game. SandboxOptions carries elecShut / elecShutModifier and waterShut / waterShutModifier; the Apocalypse defaults are mode 2 with modifier 14, so on a default save the grid goes inside the first month, centred near day 14.

Worse, the live state is trivial to read and we read neither. IsoGridSquare.haveElectricity() answers "is there power in this room"; IsoWorld.instance.isHydroPowerOn() answers "is the water grid still up". The night the power dies is one of the two or three biggest beats in a Zomboid campaign, and today it reaches the narrator only as an absence it has no instrument to detect.

Fix: add both live booleans to the snapshot and both sandbox windows to sandbox(). Then Delta gets the beat for free — power on last page, power off this page, and the interval block says so in the one place the narrator is told to look first.
Done: utilities.powerHere and utilities.mainsWater are in every snapshot, both shutoff windows are in the sandbox block, and Delta.grid() turns the moment either flips into a headline in the block the charter tells the narrator to read first.
Still openPriority 2Scenario-criticaljavap

The One Year scenario cannot see time passing

The survival spine's stated arc is attrition, and it asks the narrator to notice repetition and small changes inside it. The main visual evidence that months have gone by is the world going green over everything — the entire zombie.erosion package, with erosionSpeed and erosionDays in the sandbox. None of it reaches the prompt, so a base at month eight is described the same way it was at week one.

Partly done in 1.15.0: erosionSpeed is now in the sandbox block, so the narrator knows how fast the world reclaims itself — but nothing reads the live erosion state, so a base at month eight still looks like week one. Off-grid infrastructure is the other half of this, and that part is done: World.RULES gained an OFF THE GRID paragraph on rain barrels, plumbing and generators — a generator framed as a trade rather than a solution, light and a cold fridge in exchange for a noise that says somebody is here.

The original finding read: A generator is loud, fuelled, breakable and changes the threat picture around a base — and it is absent from World.RULES entirely. Same for rain collectors and plumbing, which are the direct answer to a water grid the narrator does not know can fail.

Meta-events & auditory migration

§7
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1Absent entirelyjavapApocalypse.lua

The one set piece built for a narrator, and it is not in the prompt

The helicopter is a scheduled, multi-day, audible event that follows the player and drags the local dead across grid cells into their walls. Slow dread with a cause you can hear coming. It is the most narratable thing in the game and the word does not appear anywhere in the mod's prompt. SandboxOptions.helicopter exists and defaults to 2 — "Sometimes" — on Apocalypse.

SandboxOptions.metaEvent is the same story: the distant gunshots, screams and dog barks that the world generates on its own. World.RULES tells the narrator the dead follow sound, but never that sound happens unprompted. Under Rule 1 — never invent into the world — an off-screen gunshot is therefore not merely unmentioned, it is forbidden. We have accidentally banned the game's own ambience.

The honest limit: I went looking for a live accessor and there is none. ChopperCommand is a server admin command; nothing exposes "is a helicopter overhead right now". So the fix is two-part, and the second part is the interesting one. Put the possibility into World.KNOX so the narrator may write a distant engine when the state shows the dead suddenly redistributing — and treat the player's note channel as the sensor. The player heard it; the player tells the device. That is not a workaround, it is the charter working as designed: the player is intent, and for this class of event the player is the only instrument we have.
Done: World.RULES gained a THE WORLD MAKES ITS OWN NOISE paragraph covering off-screen gunshots, screams, dogs, car alarms and the helicopter, with the standing limits: you may write a sound she heard, you may not invent its cause, and you may not walk her toward it. helicopter and metaEvent are now in the sandbox block so the narrator knows whether they happen in this world at all.

Engine, sandboxing & build milestones

§8
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 2Coveragejavap

Eleven options read, a hundred and eighty-one available

StateReader.sandbox() reads zombie speed, strength, toughness, transmission, mortality, cognition, memory, sight, hearing, population and day length. The framing is right — "where they differ from the ordinary rules above, THESE win" — and the reflection on getValueTranslation() means a renamed option costs a line rather than the block. Good design.

SandboxOptions declares 181 options. Most are irrelevant to prose. About a dozen are not, and every one of them has already come up in this audit: the four start-date fields, elecShut, waterShut, helicopter, metaEvent, alarm, carAlarm, erosionSpeed, sirenShutoffHours, plus zombieRespawn and lootRespawn — the last two because World.RULES asserts flatly that nothing is renewable, which is simply false on a save where they are switched on.

Done: Nine more added — both shutoffs, house and car alarms, erosion speed, helicopters, meta-events, zombie respawn and loot respawn — plus a fallback so integer options report their value instead of being dropped. ATTRITION now defers to them rather than asserting nothing is renewable.
Still openPriority 3Scope call

Most of this subject should stay out, and that is the finding

Steam Workshop support, the engine's architecture and the build history are facts about the software, not the world. Putting them in the prompt invites precisely the meta-commentary the charter bans in the same breath as bullet points. Answering the checklist honestly: this is not a gap, it is a boundary, and it should be defended.

Two narrow exceptions. The snapshot's modVersion is our version — the game build should be in there too, because B42 changed what exists in the world (crafting overhaul, basements, animals, the new map packs) and a narrator writing B42 prose against B41 assumptions will place things that are not there. And you have two Workshop mods loaded on this machine; if either adds items, their names will appear in the state with no frame around them. Low priority, but a bare mods list would stop the narrator being surprised by its own inventory.

Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 1DuplicationSource read

The campaign spine is sent twice on every request

Campaign.fixedSpine() emits the scenario spine, the premise and the opening into the cached system block. Campaign.history() then emits the same three things again into the cached prefix. Every page pays for both. Worse, the two copies disagree: fixedSpine() heads the premise "WHY SHE IS DOING THIS" and history() heads it "WHY HE IS DOING THIS".

Fix: strip the spine, premise and opening out of history() and leave it to do the one job its name promises — canon, places seen, and the pages.
Done: history() no longer emits the spine, premise or opening. It carries what grows: places seen, canon, and the pages.
Fixed in 1.15.0Priority 2RegressionSource read

The gender leak came back in Campaign.java

You caught this once in Scenario.java and it was fixed wholesale there. Three headings in Campaign.java were missed: "WHY SHE IS DOING THIS", "WHY HE IS DOING THIS" and "### PLACES HE HAS SEEN". A male survivor currently gets SHE in the system block and HE in the prefix on the same request, which is the loudest available way to make the model doubt the pronoun line it was given twice elsewhere.

Done: All nine headings in Campaign.java rewritten to the survivor / they.
